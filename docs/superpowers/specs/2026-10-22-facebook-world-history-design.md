# Facebook-first world history

Approved scope: broaden Histold to engaging evergreen world history, prioritize its monetized Facebook audience, keep two daily uploads and the existing YouTube/Instagram distribution, and commit/push without dispatching publishing jobs.

New automatic production Shorts reserve a deterministic region/series/duration assignment under the existing stage-creation lock. Nine region groups and three series interleave; two duration cycles cover all region/series/duration combinations. The first 56 reserved assignments form an exploratory four-week study. Dry previews do not consume a publishing assignment; failed production reservations remain reserved to avoid repeated subjects. Existing stages keep their snapshots, manual topics remain fixed, other channels stay unchanged.

The research brief favors curiosity, relevant context and a complete payoff over anniversaries. All source, dispute, citation and upload-quality checks remain required. Expanded approved institutions are starting points, not automatic verification. Manifest-derived map IDs, provenance checks and separate world-map viewports prevent unsupported invented geography.

Facebook uses short platform-specific captions, a recognizable series and occasional follow invitations after the answer. A Facebook failure blocks success for primary Shorts. Successful platform IDs survive retries and Facebook-only publication is recorded durably before secondary YouTube work. No automated comments, page edits or new publication is part of this code deployment.

Facebook observations remain separate from other platforms and from financial reporting periods. Age snapshots are immutable per source/definition; unequal-age native Lifetime exports stay in ignored private baselines. Net follows never stand in for gross attributed follows. Unknown metric definitions cannot produce pooled conversion rates. Evaluate watch time, shares, follows and reported earnings with scope/sample limitations; no causal winner selection from sparse data.

The supplied historical export informs test priorities but is not committed. Lifetime demographics support accessible English and readable visuals; they are not current regional reach. The existing two daily schedule times remain until comparable timing evidence is available.
