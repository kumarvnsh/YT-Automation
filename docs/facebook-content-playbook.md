## Content and presentation

Rotate India, African countries, Japan, Russia and its historical contexts, the Middle East, Europe, the Americas, and other Asian/Pacific countries. Name actual cultures and periods. Do not treat continents or regions as single cultures. Research the specific claim, not a national stereotype. Modern map outlines establish location; they never establish an ancient empire's boundaries.

Interleave three series: everyday origins and cross-cultural connections; evidence-led misconceptions and overlooked people; documented decisions and near-misses. The last preserves a useful exploratory signal in the supplied historical export without assuming that series is a proven winner. Older scripts, unfinished stages and manual topic overrides retain their existing contracts.

Start with the specific surprise in the first few spoken words. Show the relevant object, location or relationship immediately. Explain enough context for someone unfamiliar with the subject. Give a complete payoff within the video. Some endings invite a follow after the answer; others can ask one thoughtful story-specific question. No withheld answers, manufactured myths, "comment YES", forced outrage or algorithm tricks.

Use explanatory maps, diagrams and comparisons. Preserve large legible captions, high contrast and deliberate pacing; avoid rapid flashes and dense label piles. Generated reconstructions must not pretend to be historical evidence. Background findings excluded by research cannot enter narration.

The 30-second concise profile uses five beats; the 50-second story profile uses seven. These are prospective tests. Do not stretch a simple fact or compress an explanation until its qualifications disappear. The generator and quality gate read the same snapshotted duration. Stop and inspect research/quality failures rather than weakening evidence requirements.
